Lesson 1 — How LLMs Actually Work
Conceptual foundation · No math required
45 min

You need to understand what a large language model is — not mathematically, but conceptually. This shapes how you prompt, what you trust, and where you spot failure modes. Key concepts: tokens, context windows, the difference between training and inference, and why models "hallucinate."

Lesson 2 — Prompt Engineering Fundamentals
Highest-leverage skill · Applies immediately
60 min

The single skill that multiplies everything else. For product and engineering roles, good prompts are the difference between a usable spec and a generic one, a useful code review and a shallow one. Learn: zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, role-based prompting, and format specification.

Lesson 3 — Workflow Audit: Find Your Top 3 AI Opportunities
Interactive scoring tool below ↓
90 min

Before touching any tool, map your actual work. Score each task you do regularly across four dimensions: frequency, time consumed, repeatability, and language-heaviness. This surfaces your real highest-ROI targets — not generic AI use cases, but your use cases.

For product and engineering roles, common high-scorers include: writing PRDs and specs, summarizing user research, writing release notes, reviewing architecture decisions, drafting engineering proposals, and generating test cases.
